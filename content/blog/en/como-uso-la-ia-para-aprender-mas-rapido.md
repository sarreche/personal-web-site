Artificial intelligence has changed the way I approach a new subject. Not because it can learn for me, but because it helps remove some of the friction that often comes with studying: not knowing where to begin, wasting time looking for the right explanation, or moving forward without a clear structure.

When I use it well, AI becomes a combination of tutor, study guide, and practice partner. These are the techniques I use to make the most of it.

## 1. I start with a concrete outcome

Before asking for an explanation, I try to define what I want to be able to do.

“I want to learn programming” is too broad. “I want to understand enough Python to automate a repetitive task” gives the learning process a direction.

The clearer the expected outcome, the better AI can help you choose what to study, in which order, and at what depth.

## 2. I ask it to adjust the explanation

The same idea can be explained in many ways. If the first answer feels too technical, I ask for a simpler version. If I already know the fundamentals, I ask it to skip the basics and go deeper.

Some instructions I often use are:

- “Explain it as if I were just getting started.”
- “Now explain it with more technical precision.”
- “Use an analogy from everyday life.”
- “What background knowledge do I need to understand this?”.

I do not have to adapt to a single explanation. I can adjust the explanation until I find one that helps me move forward.

## 3. I turn the conversation into questions

Reading an answer and feeling that you understood it does not always mean you can use that knowledge.

That is why I ask AI to question me one prompt at a time and wait for my answer before continuing. I can also ask it to identify my mistakes without immediately revealing the solution.

This turns passive reading into active recall: I have to retrieve the answer from memory, explain what I understood, and discover where my real gaps are.

## 4. I ask for examples and practice

When an idea still feels abstract, I ask for examples. Then I try to solve a similar case on my own.

AI can generate exercises of increasing difficulty, realistic situations, or small variations of the same problem. It can also examine my answer and point out which part of my reasoning needs work.

The goal is not to watch AI solve everything. It is to use AI to create more opportunities to practice.

## 5. I use AI to find gaps

One useful technique is to explain the subject in my own words and ask AI to find inaccuracies, assumptions, or concepts I missed.

I can also ask:

- “Which part of my explanation is ambiguous?”.
- “What counterexample would test this idea?”.
- “What question should I be able to answer if I truly understand the subject?”.

An AI response is not a final authority. For important subjects, I verify the information against documentation, books, or reliable sources. But as a tool for comparison, it can quickly show me where I need to go deeper.

## 6. I ask for a system, not just a goal

A goal tells you what you want to achieve. A system helps you show up and make progress every day.

For example, I can ask:

> “Create a 14-day study plan with 20-minute sessions and one clear objective for each day.”

Seeing the path divided into small, ordered steps reduces the feeling of facing something enormous. Every session has a purpose, and progress becomes visible.

The plan does not have to be perfect. After a few days, I can explain what felt easy, what was difficult, and how much time I have available so it can adjust the plan.

## 7. I adapt the format to the moment

I do not always need to learn in the same way. Sometimes a diagram makes a complex relationship clear. At other times, I would rather listen to the main ideas, read an example, or try something with my own hands.

AI can represent the same material in different forms:

- An outline or diagram to visualize relationships.
- A short script that can be heard as audio.
- A practical example that turns theory into action.
- A table that compares concepts.
- Three main ideas when I am tired or short on time.

This is not about placing myself inside a fixed label such as “visual” or “auditory.” It is about choosing the format that best fits the subject, the objective, and the energy I have at that moment.

## Study better, not only more

AI does not learn for you. It cannot replace practice, curiosity, or the work of building an idea inside your own mind.

What it can do is save time, frustration, and unnecessary detours. It can help you find an entry point, organize the path, practice with intention, and receive immediate feedback.

The point is not to study more. It is to study better.

Try it today with any subject you want to understand. Define what you want to achieve, ask for an explanation that fits your starting point, and turn the conversation into practice. You may be surprised by how much progress you can make in a short time.
